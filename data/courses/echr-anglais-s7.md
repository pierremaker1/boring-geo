## Les écarts entre ton cours et le droit positif

Classés du plus important au plus anecdotique.

1. **[tim-001]** Positive law: the time limit is now FOUR months (Protocol No. 15, applicable since 1 February 2022). The course has not been updated.
2. **[crt-001]** The printed handout still says 47, before Russia ceased to be a member in 2022.
3. **[fnd-009]** The handout 'Guide for the Civil & Public Service' still says 47. The correct current figure is 46.
4. **[fnd-010]** The handout still says 28 (pre-Brexit).
5. **[prot-005]** The course says Turkey has not ratified Protocol 13. Turkey in fact ratified it on 20 February 2006 (in force 1 June 2006); today Azerbaijan is the only Council of Europe member not to have ratified it. The rule the example illustrates (a protocol binds only its ratifying States) remains correct.
6. **[trp-003]** Two inaccuracies carried over from the course. Article 2 of Protocol 6 allows the death penalty only for acts committed in time of war or of imminent threat of war, not for treason. And Turkey did ratify Protocol 13, in 2006.
7. **[prot-004]** The course adds 'treason' to the Protocol 6 exceptions. Article 2 of Protocol 6 in fact covers only acts committed in time of war or of imminent threat of war. Answer as taught, but know the text.
8. **[art-015]** The handout states that 'the government cannot restrict political activity simply because a person is not a citizen'. Article 16 in fact PERMITS such restrictions; it is the Court that reads the provision very narrowly (Piermont v France, 1995). Answer as taught, but do not repeat the handout's gloss in an essay.
9. **[adm-004]** The lecture notes go further and say the respondent State will not know who the applicant is. Under Rule 47 the applicant may ask that their identity not be disclosed TO THE PUBLIC; the respondent State necessarily knows it. Answer as taught, but do not write this in an essay.
10. **[tim-005]** As taught. Under Rule 47 §6(a) of the Rules of Court as currently in force, the relevant date is the date of DISPATCH (the postmark), not the date of receipt. The course has not been updated on this point.
11. **[trp-016]** As taught: e-mail and fax do not constitute valid lodging. On the dispatch-versus-receipt question, see the flag on the time-limit questions: the Rules of Court now look to the postmark.
12. **[crt-005]** As taught. In strict terms a single judge may only declare an application inadmissible or strike it out; only a three-judge committee may rule on the merits of a matter covered by well-established case law (article 28).
13. **[crt-008]** As taught, and as required by annales Question 14. In strict terms the Court's judgments are declaratory: the State chooses the general measures, under the supervision of the Committee of Ministers, except in pilot judgments where the Court indicates them.
14. **[nat-003]** The printed handout lists only articles 3, 4(1) and 7 as absolute. The lecture notes and the course plan add article 2. Follow the plan: 2, 3, 4, 7.
15. **[prot-011]** The handout says Protocol 12 'will enter into force when ratified by ten Contracting States'. It in fact entered into force on 1 April 2005, but only for the States that have ratified it.
16. **[prot-012]** Careful: the example is out of date. Turkey ratified Protocol 13 in 2006. Azerbaijan is now the only member State not to have ratified it. The principle itself is unaffected.
17. **[trp-021]** The course uses Turkey as its example of a State that has not ratified Protocol 13. Turkey ratified it in 2006; Azerbaijan is the correct current example.
18. **[p11-004]** The course handout attributes the violation to a difference in treatment based on the applicant's Serbian ethnicity. The Court in fact found a disproportionate interference with the peaceful enjoyment of possessions (no repossession and no compensation) and did not examine article 14 separately.
19. **[a8-004]** Course materials date Dudgeon 1982; the judgment was delivered on 22 October 1981. The handout also dates Modinos v Cyprus 1994, whereas it was 22 April 1993, and Kokkinakis 1994, whereas it was 25 May 1993.
20. **[a9-002]** The handout dates this case 1994; the judgment was delivered on 25 May 1993.
21. **[a12-002]** Course materials date this case both 1986 and 1987. The judgment was delivered on 18 December 1986.
22. **[a6-006]** The handout dates this case 2007; the judgment was in fact delivered on 14 November 2006.
